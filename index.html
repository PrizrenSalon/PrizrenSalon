The Story

It started as a casual summer dinner with friends — Christian Lorentzen, Lily Lynch, Mikra Namani, Valdete Berisha, and Michael Barron — getting together to talk literature and stuff, drink and eat until late, and now we’re here. At the heart of it the Prizren Literary Salon, founded by Mikra Namani and Valdete Berisha, is about literature, reading, and writing. It brings together regional and international writers and poets in Prizren, Kosovo, for a two-day visit which coincides with Dokufest, an international documentary and short film festival. The central focus of the visit is a literary evening organized on the second day, featuring several writers reading from their work — novels, essays, poetry, and more. Last year’s edition welcomed writers, novelists, poets, translators, and critics, including Christian Lorentzen (NYC), Ryan Ruby (Berlin), Josh Furst (NYC), Suzana Vuljevic (Chicago), and Kosovar poet Ervina Halili.

The reading is open to the public and will take place on Saturday, Aug. 8, at 18:00, at the iconic BarAca. Readings are primarily in English.

Venue

Prizren,
Kosovo

Prizren is Kosovo’s second largest city, located on the slopes of the Sharr Mountains in the southwest of the country. First mentioned in records in 1019 during the reign of Emperor Basil II, the city has served for centuries as a crossroads of cultures, faiths, and languages. Its preserved Ottoman old town, medieval fortress, and landmarks such as the Sinan Pasha Mosque and the UNESCO World Heritage Site of Our Lady of Ljeviš make it one of the most historically rich cities in the Western Balkans.
